# Purpose

This document defines the permanent engineering contracts governing the core foundation of Limoxel.

Core Contracts establish the stable agreements through which the Runtime, Infrastructure, Engineering Components, and future platform capabilities collaborate while preserving architectural consistency.

These contracts define engineering expectations.

They do not define implementation.

The purpose of Core Contracts is to ensure that every permanent engineering capability introduced into Limoxel participates within a predictable, stable, and maintainable engineering foundation.

---

# Definition of a Core Contract

Within Limoxel, a Core Contract represents a permanent engineering agreement governing collaboration between engineering capabilities.

Core Contracts define responsibilities, expectations, participation rules, and lifecycle obligations.

They intentionally avoid implementation details.

Implementations satisfy Core Contracts.

They do not redefine them.

---

# Objectives

Core Contracts exist to achieve the following objectives.

- Engineering consistency.
- Stable collaboration.
- Independent implementation.
- Predictable platform behavior.
- Long-term maintainability.
- Controlled engineering evolution.
- Production reliability.

Every permanent engineering capability introduced into Limoxel shall comply with the Core Contracts established by this document.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

Engineering capabilities should collaborate through permanent agreements rather than implementation assumptions.

Contracts should remain stable while implementations evolve.

The engineering foundation should encourage replacement, refinement, optimization, and future expansion without requiring redesign of permanent engineering agreements.

Stable contracts contribute directly to long-term platform sustainability.

---

# Contract Responsibilities

Every Core Contract shall define one well-defined engineering responsibility.

Responsibilities should remain cohesive.

Contracts representing unrelated engineering concerns should remain separate.

Engineering responsibilities shall remain understandable without reference to implementation.

---

# Contract Ownership

Every Core Contract shall possess one clearly identifiable owner.

Ownership defines responsibility for the evolution, consistency, and long-term maintenance of the contract.

Consumers may depend upon a contract.

They shall not redefine it.

Ownership shall remain consistent with the architectural ownership established throughout Phase 1.

---

# Contract Independence

Core Contracts shall remain independent from implementation technologies, engineering frameworks, infrastructure choices, and future roadmap phases.

A Core Contract should remain valid even if every implementation satisfying that contract is replaced.

Contract independence preserves long-term engineering flexibility.

---

# Contract Minimalism

Core Contracts should define only the engineering obligations required for stable collaboration.

Engineering agreements should remain intentionally focused.

Unnecessary expansion of contract responsibilities should be avoided.

Smaller, well-defined contracts improve engineering clarity, implementation flexibility, maintainability, and long-term platform evolution.

---

# Contract Stability

Core Contracts represent one of the most stable engineering assets within Limoxel.

Implementation improvements should rarely require modification of permanent contracts.

Whenever contract evolution becomes necessary, changes should provide demonstrable long-term engineering benefit.

Stable contracts contribute directly to production reliability.

---

# Engineering Expectations

Every Core Contract introduced into Limoxel shall:

- Define one engineering responsibility.
- Preserve architectural consistency.
- Remain implementation independent.
- Support long-term maintainability.
- Encourage engineering clarity.
- Enable future evolution.
- Strengthen the engineering foundation.

Core Contracts shall remain stable throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

---

# Contract Collaboration

Core Contracts enable engineering capabilities to collaborate while preserving architectural independence.

Collaboration shall occur through clearly defined engineering agreements rather than implementation assumptions.

Core Contracts shall preserve:

- Component ownership.
- Package organization.
- Dependency direction.
- Interface architecture.
- Infrastructure architecture.

Engineering collaboration should remain predictable, understandable, and maintainable.

---

# Contract Lifecycle

Every permanent Core Contract should progress through a controlled engineering lifecycle.

This lifecycle should include:

- Engineering identification.
- Responsibility definition.
- Contract design.
- Engineering review.
- Approval.
- Production implementation.
- Validation.
- Long-term maintenance.

Core Contracts shall become permanent engineering assets only after successfully completing this lifecycle.

---

# Contract Evolution

Core Contracts are expected to evolve throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

Such evolution should preserve engineering consistency while enabling future platform capabilities.

Contract evolution should emphasize:

- Stability.
- Predictability.
- Simplicity.
- Long-term maintainability.

Whenever contract evolution introduces engineering uncertainty, engineering review shall occur before implementation proceeds.

---

# Contract Compatibility

Core Contracts should preserve compatibility whenever reasonably practical.

Engineering improvements should minimize unnecessary disruption to dependent engineering capabilities.

When compatibility cannot reasonably be preserved, engineering justification shall exist before changes are adopted.

Compatibility contributes directly to production stability.

---

# Contract Reusability

Core Contracts should encourage engineering reuse.

Equivalent engineering responsibilities should utilize existing permanent contracts whenever appropriate.

Creation of additional contracts should occur only when existing engineering agreements can no longer reasonably represent future engineering responsibilities.

Contract reuse should strengthen engineering clarity rather than increase unnecessary abstraction.

---

# Contract Maintainability

Core Contracts should remain understandable throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

Maintenance activities shall preserve:

- Responsibility clarity.
- Engineering ownership.
- Contract consistency.
- Architectural alignment.
- Long-term stability.

Maintainability shall remain a permanent engineering objective.

Every Core Contract should demonstrate the following engineering qualities.

- Clarity.
- Stability.
- Simplicity.
- Predictability.
- Consistency.
- Maintainability.
- Reusability.
- Extensibility.

Engineering quality shall remain the defining characteristic of every permanent contract introduced into Limoxel.

# Contract Integrity

Core Contracts shall preserve the integrity of the engineering foundation.

Contracts shall never weaken:

- Component boundaries.
- Dependency relationships.
- Communication principles.
- Interface architecture.
- Infrastructure architecture.
- Runtime architecture.

Engineering contracts shall strengthen the platform rather than introduce architectural complexity.

Whenever contract modifications threaten engineering integrity, implementation shall pause until engineering review has been completed.

# Long-Term Vision

The Core Contracts established during Phase 1 are intended to remain permanent engineering agreements throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

Future roadmap phases may introduce additional engineering capabilities, integrations, execution models, and platform services.

These future capabilities should integrate through the permanent Core Contracts established during Phase 1 rather than introducing incompatible engineering agreements.

Stable contracts contribute directly to the long-term sustainability, maintainability, and production reliability of Limoxel.

---

# Contract Anti-Patterns

The following engineering practices should be avoided.

- Contracts without clear ownership.
- Duplicate engineering agreements.
- Contracts exposing implementation details.
- Contracts representing unrelated responsibilities.
- Frequent contract redesign.
- Contracts created solely for temporary implementation convenience.
- Engineering collaboration bypassing permanent contracts.

Whenever these situations occur, engineering review shall be completed before implementation proceeds.
