# Purpose

This document defines the engineering design governing the organization of the permanent production foundation of Limoxel.

The Foundation Package Map translates the architectural foundation into an engineering structure suitable for long-term production implementation.

Its purpose is to establish a stable organizational model that enables predictable growth while preserving architectural consistency, engineering clarity, and long-term maintainability.

The Foundation Package Map serves as the bridge between engineering design and production implementation.

---

# Definition of the Foundation Package Map

Within Limoxel, the Foundation Package Map represents the permanent organizational blueprint from which the engineering foundation is implemented.

It describes how engineering responsibilities are translated into production packages while preserving the architectural principles established throughout Phase 1.

The Foundation Package Map governs engineering organization.

It does not define implementation.

---

# Objectives

The Foundation Package Map exists to achieve the following objectives.

- Stable engineering organization.
- Predictable production structure.
- Clear engineering ownership.
- Long-term maintainability.
- Controlled engineering growth.
- Architectural consistency.
- Production readiness.

Every production package introduced into Limoxel shall ultimately conform to the principles established by this document.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

The engineering foundation should grow through deliberate organization rather than organic expansion.

Every production package should exist because it represents a permanent engineering responsibility.

Package creation shall always follow engineering necessity rather than implementation convenience.

The engineering foundation should remain understandable throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

---

# Organizational Philosophy

Production packages exist to organize engineering responsibilities.

Organization shall reflect architectural ownership rather than temporary implementation requirements.

The engineering structure should encourage:

- High cohesion.
- Low coupling.
- Independent evolution.
- Engineering clarity.
- Long-term maintainability.

The organizational philosophy established during Phase 1 is intended to remain stable throughout future roadmap phases.

---

# Engineering Responsibilities

Every production package shall possess one primary engineering responsibility.

Packages should remain cohesive throughout their lifetime.

Engineering capabilities that naturally belong together should remain together.

Engineering capabilities representing unrelated concerns should remain separated.

Responsibility ownership shall remain explicit and stable.

---

# Foundation Stability

The production foundation established during Phase 1 shall remain significantly more stable than the engineering capabilities introduced during future roadmap phases.

Future engineering work should primarily extend the existing organizational foundation rather than repeatedly reorganize it.

Structural redesign should remain exceptional.

---

# Package Ownership

Every production package shall possess one clearly identifiable owner.

Ownership defines responsibility for the engineering concepts implemented within the package.

Ownership shall remain consistent with:

- Component Boundaries.
- Package Structure.
- Interface Architecture.

Package ownership shall never become ambiguous.

---

# Engineering Expectations

Every production package introduced into Limoxel shall:

- Possess one primary engineering responsibility.
- Preserve architectural consistency.
- Remain independently maintainable.
- Support future engineering growth.
- Minimize unnecessary coupling.
- Maximize cohesion.
- Strengthen the engineering foundation.

The Foundation Package Map should remain one of the most stable engineering assets within Limoxel.

# Engineering Organization

The Foundation Package Map establishes the permanent organizational model for the engineering foundation of Limoxel.

Engineering organization shall remain a direct reflection of architectural responsibilities.

Production organization should strengthen engineering understanding while preserving long-term maintainability.

Organization shall evolve deliberately rather than organically.

---

# Responsibility Mapping

Every architectural responsibility should ultimately be represented by an appropriate production organization.

The translation from architecture to production implementation shall remain consistent, predictable, and understandable.

Production organization shall preserve the ownership, dependency, communication, and extension principles established throughout the architectural foundation.

---

# Organizational Hierarchy

The engineering foundation shall remain organized through logical responsibility rather than implementation convenience.

Higher organizational levels should represent broader engineering responsibilities.

Lower organizational levels should represent progressively more specialized engineering responsibilities.

Organizational hierarchy shall improve engineering understanding without introducing unnecessary complexity.

---

# Organizational Minimalism

The engineering foundation should introduce only the organizational structure necessary to represent permanent engineering responsibilities.

New production organization should not be created solely to accommodate temporary implementation requirements.

A smaller, well-organized engineering foundation improves readability, maintainability, onboarding, and long-term evolution.

Organizational growth should remain intentional rather than incremental.

---

# Organizational Boundaries

Engineering organization shall preserve clear organizational boundaries.

Responsibilities belonging to different engineering concerns should remain separated whenever practical.

Organizational boundaries shall prevent engineering ownership from becoming ambiguous.

Whenever organizational boundaries begin weakening architectural clarity, engineering review shall occur before implementation proceeds.

---

# Organizational Cohesion

Engineering organization should maximize cohesion.

Capabilities that naturally contribute toward the same engineering responsibility should remain together whenever appropriate.

High cohesion improves:

- Readability.
- Maintainability.
- Testing.
- Future evolution.
- Engineering understanding.

---

# Organizational Coupling

Engineering organization should minimize unnecessary coupling.

Relationships between production packages should exist only when justified by architectural responsibility.

Organizational convenience shall never justify unnecessary engineering dependencies.

Reducing coupling contributes directly to long-term maintainability and platform stability.

---

# Organizational Growth

The engineering foundation is expected to grow continuously throughout the lifetime of Limoxel.

Growth should occur through controlled expansion of the organizational structure while preserving the engineering foundation established during Phase 1.

New engineering responsibilities should integrate naturally into the existing organizational model.

Growth should strengthen organization rather than increase structural complexity.

---

# Organizational Evolution

As Limoxel evolves, the organizational structure may expand to accommodate new engineering capabilities.

Expansion should preserve:

- Architectural consistency.
- Responsibility ownership.
- Organizational clarity.
- Engineering maintainability.

Organizational evolution shall emphasize refinement rather than restructuring.

# Organizational Stability

The engineering organization established during Phase 1 should remain sufficiently stable to support future roadmap phases without repeated structural redesign.

Future roadmap phases should primarily introduce new engineering capabilities within the existing organizational framework.

Structural redesign should remain exceptional and require architectural justification.

Engineering organization should remain understandable.

The Foundation Package Map established during Phase 1 is intended to remain the permanent organizational blueprint supporting Limoxel throughout its lifetime.

# Organizational Anti-Patterns

The following engineering practices should be avoided.

- Production organization driven by temporary implementation needs.
- Packages without clear ownership.
- Duplicate organizational responsibilities.
- Frequent structural redesign.
- Organizational growth without architectural justification.
- Engineering responsibilities distributed across unrelated production packages.
- Structural complexity introduced without long-term engineering value.

Whenever these situations occur, engineering review shall be completed before implementation proceeds.
