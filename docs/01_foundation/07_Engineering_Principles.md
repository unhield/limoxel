# Purpose

This document defines the official Engineering Principles of Limoxel.

Engineering principles establish the fundamental philosophy that guides how Limoxel is designed, implemented, maintained, and evolved. They provide a consistent framework for making technical decisions, resolving engineering trade-offs, and preserving the long-term quality of the platform.

As a Core Foundation Document, this document serves as the canonical source of truth for engineering practices throughout the lifecycle of Limoxel.

---

# Engineering Principles

The engineering philosophy of Limoxel is founded on the belief that long-term engineering excellence is achieved through thoughtful design, simplicity, reliability, and disciplined decision-making rather than rapid feature development.

Every engineering decision should strengthen the platform's reliability, maintainability, and long-term value.

---

# Principle Interpretation

## 1. Engineering Before Features

Engineering quality always takes precedence over feature quantity.

Features should only be introduced when they solve meaningful engineering problems without compromising the integrity of the platform.

---

## 2. Solve Real Engineering Problems

Every capability developed within Limoxel should address a genuine software engineering challenge.

Engineering effort should focus on creating practical, lasting value rather than pursuing temporary industry trends.

---

## 3. Think Long Term

Engineering decisions should prioritize sustainability over short-term convenience.

Designs should remain understandable, maintainable, and extensible for years rather than optimizing solely for immediate implementation.

---

## 4. Simplicity Enables Reliability

Complexity should only be introduced when it delivers clear and measurable value.

Whenever multiple solutions exist, preference should be given to the simplest solution that satisfies the engineering requirements.

---

## 5. Reliability Is a Feature

Reliability is not an enhancement—it is a fundamental capability.

Every component should behave consistently, predictably, and correctly under expected operating conditions.

---

## 6. Stable Public Interfaces

Public interfaces should evolve carefully.

Backward compatibility should be preserved whenever reasonably possible, and breaking changes should be introduced only when they provide substantial long-term benefit.

---

## 7. Open by Design

Limoxel is committed to open engineering.

Design decisions, documentation, architecture, and implementation should remain transparent whenever practical, encouraging learning, collaboration, and community participation.

---

## 8. Performance Through Good Design

Performance should result from sound architecture and efficient implementation rather than premature optimization.

Optimizations should be guided by evidence, measurement, and real engineering requirements.

---

## 9. Build Modular Systems

The platform should be composed of well-defined, loosely coupled, and highly cohesive components.

Modularity improves maintainability, extensibility, testing, and long-term evolution.

---

## 10. Design for Testability

Every component should be designed to support effective verification.

Testing should be considered during design rather than added as an afterthought.

---

## 11. Documentation Is Engineering

Documentation is an essential engineering artifact.

Every significant architectural decision, public interface, and major capability should be documented clearly enough to support long-term understanding and maintenance.

---

## 12. Respect the Community

Engineering decisions should support a healthy open-source ecosystem.

Contributions, discussions, and reviews should strengthen both the platform and the engineering community surrounding it.

---

## 13. AI as an Engineering Collaborator

Limoxel is designed to empower AI systems through verified engineering intelligence rather than treating AI as a replacement for software engineers.

Human expertise remains central to engineering decision-making.

---

## 14. Build Once, Build Well

Every implementation should strive for production quality from its initial design.

Technical debt should be minimized, and engineering shortcuts should never become permanent architecture.

---

## 15. Protect the North Star

Every engineering decision should reinforce Limoxel's mission, vision, and long-term North Star.

When engineering trade-offs arise, preference should always be given to the solution that best supports the project's long-term direction.

- System design
- Implementation
- Code reviews
- Testing
- Documentation
- Public APIs
- Plugin ecosystem
- Performance optimization
- Security
- Community contributions
- Repository governance

Every engineering decision should remain consistent with these principles.

# Foundational Principles

The Engineering Principles establish the following permanent commitments.

- Engineering quality over feature quantity.
- Long-term sustainability over short-term convenience.
- Simplicity over unnecessary complexity.
- Reliability over rapid delivery.
- Verified engineering decisions over assumptions.
- Modularity over tightly coupled systems.
- Documentation as a core engineering responsibility.
- Performance through thoughtful architecture.
- Collaboration through openness and transparency.
- Continuous alignment with Limoxel's mission, vision, and North Star.
